Remaining limits
- Audio physically erased before migration cannot be recovered. It is permanently ineligible. Old durable clips with no trustworthy surviving manifest stay pending even when their clip audio remains playable; this run deliberately refuses to trust their cached IDs retrospectively.
- The legacy recovery path needs the existing approved 512-float embedding and complete audio coverage. It does not reconstruct missing biometric vectors from a newly loaded model. Temporary key failures defer recovery, potentially retaining affected recording ciphertext past its usual expiry until a later successful startup.
- Historical erasure intent cannot be reconstructed if an import discarded both durable ownership/format information and its explicit erase reason: an otherwise identical vector-only removal is indistinguishable from a genuinely reversible legacy undo. Such ambiguous snapshots are preserved; their vectors are still ineligible for recognition without new provenance and durable evidence. The >110-history probe covers the reproduced case with surviving ownership snapshots, independently of the pruned marker.
- Tests prove logical erasure from live rows and affected retained snapshots, not physical erasure from SQLite free pages, WAL files or backups.
- The browser uses mocked WAV/IPC and proves renderer availability, disclosure, responsive fit and playback wiring. Helper fixtures separately use SQLite/filesystem storage, injected AES-GCM and a fixture codec. Production codec, native/system-keychain recovery and actual speaker audio output were not exercised.
- Translation keys/interpolation pass coverage in all locales; native-speaker linguistic review was not performed.
- No deployment, restart of a running service, push, PR or release was performed.
